Introduction¶

The goal of this project is to build a machine learning model that predicts whether a Formula 1 driver will finish on the podium, meaning in the top three, in a Grand Prix. This is therefor a Binary Classificatio problem because each driver result is classified as either a podium finish or a non-podium finish

The data for this project comes from the Formula 1 World Championship (1950–2024) dataset on Kaggle. It includes race results, drivers, lap times, and other relavent race-related data that would be helpfull to train the model to create accurate predictions. This data set includes many csv files, which can be joined with keys like raceID and driverID.

This dataset includes both categorical and numerical variables.

Numerical: grid position, finishing position, points, laps completed, qualifying rank, and standings points

Categorical: driver name, constructor/team, circuit, race name, nationality, and race status

How does Forumula 1 Work?¶

A F1 season is made up of multiple Grand Prix weekends. During this weeking, drivers participate in practice sessions, qualifying, and the main Grand Prix race.

There are three practice sessions where the driver and the team (constructor) prepare the car, but do not determine points or positions.

Qualifying determines a drivers starting position in the Grand Prix. It is brocken up into three sessions:

- Q1 (18 minutes): All 20 drivers race. The 6 slowest drivers are eliminated and fill grid positions 16 through 20
- Q2 (15 minutes): The remaining 14 drivers compete. The 6 slowest drivers are eliminated to secure grid positions 11 through 15. 
- Q3 (12 minutes): The final 10 drivers race to determine the top 10 starting spots for the Grand Prix.

The faster the driver, the lower their starting position is (pole position). Sometimes the final starting grid can differ from qualifying order because of penalties or car changes.

The Grand Prix is the main race. Drivers earn championship points based on their finishing position, and the top three finishers are considered podium finishers.

    X_train = train[features]

Maximum depth of the individual regression estimators. The maximum

min_impurity_decrease min_impurity_decrease: float, default=0.0

A node will be split if this split induces a decrease of the impurity
greater than or equal to this value.
Values must be in the range `[0.0, inf)`.

The weighted impurity decrease equation is the following::

N_t / N * (impurity - N_t_R / N_t * right_impurity
- N_t_L / N_t * left_impurity)

where ``N`` is the total number of samples, ``N_t`` is the number of
samples at the current node, ``N_t_L`` is the number of samples in the
left child, and ``N_t_R`` is the number of samples in the right child.

``N``, ``N_t``, ``N_t_R`` and ``N_t_L`` all refer to the weighted sum,
if ``sample_weight`` is passed.
